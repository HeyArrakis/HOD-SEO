Date: 2026-09-24. Scope: the role, boundaries and operating rules of the HOD Blog (Journal), and how it relates to Education and to commercial pages. This is a strategic framework. It is not an article list, not a set of content briefs, not a keyword→URL map (Stage 15), not the Education architecture (Stage 14) and not the internal-link map (Stage 16). ## The starting point in one paragraph

OBSERVED FACT [Stage 10]: the Blog holds 10 articles and every one is an evergreen reference or buying-decision piece: lab-grown science, comparisons, 4Cs, shapes, metals, sizing and certification. None is time-bound, editorial or brand-led. The Blog holds 26 of HOD's 28 Stage 3 rankings, all at position 27 or lower, and HOD appears in 0 of 795 Stage 8 top-10 results. Stage 12 (D-09, D-10) made Education the single evergreen home and gave the Blog a different job.

**Consequence (INFERENCE):** under the new role, the Blog has **no qualifying editorial content today**. Its current articles become Education-owned. The four ranking ones keep their `/blog` URLs until migration conditions are met; see §6 and `13_content_migration_risk.csv`. The future Blog therefore starts almost from zero. Treat that as a reason for a small, deliberate launch, not for filling it quickly.

## 1. Blog purpose

PROPOSED. The Blog is HOD's **editorial and brand voice**. It exists to:

1. Show **who makes the jewellery and how**, including craftsmanship, the Surat workshop, design and bespoke work. This supplies the first-hand experience and trust evidence that reference pages cannot.
2. Cover **what is changing**: trends, seasons, launches, collections and industry developments.
3. **Carry readers into the right permanent page.** Concepts go to Education and products go to categories.

The Blog is **not** HOD's main organic-search engine for diamond questions. On the evidence, search demand for diamond concepts is informational and evergreen, and Stage 12 assigned all of it to Education. Any Blog search traffic is a by-product of time-bound or brand topics, and the Blog is judged on that basis.

## 2. What the Blog owns

The Blog is the primary home for these topic types (detail in `13_blog_education_framework.csv`):

| Editorial area | Owned because | Business support |
|---|---|---|

**Not included**, because the project has no business evidence for them: celebrity and red-carpet news, wedding-planning content beyond jewellery, and fashion content unrelated to HOD products. These can be added later if the business decides to, but the research does not support them.

## 3. What the Blog does not own

The Blog must **not** become the primary home for any of these. They belong to Education [Stage 12 D-06, D-09]:

- CVD vs HPHT, and CVD or HPHT explained on its own
- Lab-grown vs natural
- Whether lab-grown diamonds are real (authenticity and legitimacy)
- How lab-grown diamonds are made
- Diamond certification and grading reports (GIA, IGI and others)
- The 4Cs, grade scales and grade comparisons
- Permanent diamond-shape and cut education
- Metals and materials comparisons (14k vs 18k and similar)
- Permanent buying guides ("how to choose", "is it worth it", "what to look for")
- Ring sizing, care and testing/identification reference

It must also never target **shopping queries**. Category and product SERPs are commercial [Stage 8] and belong to category pages and PDPs.

A Blog post **may mention** any of these subjects in passing. When it does, it gives a one- or two-sentence answer and links to the Education owner. It does not re-explain the topic.

## 4. Blog vs Education distinction

The distinction is by **job**, not by topic [Stage 12 D-10]:

| | Education | Blog |
|---|---|---|
| Question it answers | "What is it? Which should I choose? How does it work?" | "What's new? Who made it? What's in season? What's the story?" |
| Shelf life | Permanent; updated in place | Dated; may be refreshed, archived or left as record |
| Format | Reference, comparison, guide, tool | Feature, story, announcement, trend piece, gallery |
| URL behaviour | One stable URL per topic | One URL per post; recurring seasonal posts reuse one URL |
| Success measure | Rankings and assisted conversions for evergreen queries | Engagement, links earned, brand/trust signals, assisted traffic to Education and categories |

**Four tests for any new piece** (apply in order):
1. **Shelf-life test.** Would the core answer still be correct and complete in two years without edits? If yes, it is Education.
2. **Question test.** Is it phrased as a stable question someone searches for ("what is", "vs", "how to choose")? If yes, it is Education.
3. **Ownership test.** Does an Education page already own this topic? If yes, the Blog links to it and may only cover a time-bound angle.
4. **Brand test.** Can only HOD tell this story (its workshop, its commissions, its launches)? If yes, it is Blog.

If the tests disagree, Education wins for anything a searcher would ask repeatedly, and Blog wins for anything tied to a date or to HOD itself.

## 5. Future editorial categories

PROPOSED, at category level only (no titles):

1. **Craft & Making**: workshop, techniques, setting, finishing, bespoke process
2. **Style & Trends**: seasonal trends, styling, stacking, pairing
3. **Gifts & Occasions**: recurring seasonal guides
4. **Collections & Launches**: new pieces and curated collections
5. **Stories**: brand, people, client commissions (the last is a FUTURE POSSIBILITY)
6. **Industry Notes**: dated lab-grown and jewellery market developments

Rules:
- **Do not publish an empty category page.** A category becomes a live, indexable listing only when it holds real posts (Stage 12 D-13). Until then the Blog hub is a single chronological list.
- **Categories are for navigation, not keyword targeting.** A category page is not a landing page for a search query.
- **Tags are not indexable.** Six categories are enough, and tag archives create thin duplicate listings.

## 6. Content lifecycle

1. **Plan**: run the four tests in §4. Check that no Education or commercial page already owns the subject.
2. **Publish**: include a visible date, a named author (§8), and links out to the Education owner and the relevant category.
3. **Review**:
   - Trend and industry posts: after 12 months.
   - Seasonal guides: before each season.
   - Launch posts: when the collection changes.
4. **Decide at review**, choosing one of:
   - **Refresh**: recurring seasonal content on the same URL.
   - **Keep as record**: brand and craft stories that remain true.
   - **Consolidate**: if two posts cover the same dated subject, keep one and 301 the other.
   - **Promote**: if a post turns out to answer an evergreen question (for example, it attracts steady evergreen search traffic), its substance moves into the Education owner and the post links there. The post is not reframed as a second reference page.
   - **Retire**: only with a redirect decision recorded, never a silent deletion.

**Transitional state for existing articles.** Four ranking articles are Education-owned but keep `/blog` URLs until the conditions in `13_content_migration_risk.csv` are met. They should be treated as Education pages in every respect except the path: Education template standards, Education hub links, no Blog-style dating. That is how "one topic, one home" holds without a risky early move.

## 7. Freshness and update principles

- **Education updates in place.** It keeps one URL and shows a "last reviewed" date.
- **The Blog does not chase freshness with new URLs.** No "2026 trends" and then "2027 trends" as separate URLs. A recurring subject reuses one URL, with the year in the title only, and the content is replaced each cycle.
- **The Blog does not fake freshness.** Dates are not changed without a real content change.
- **Price and market figures date quickly.** They go in dated Blog posts or are clearly dated inside Education pages (framework row: value and pricing).
- **Cadence:** no fixed publishing quota is proposed. The project has no editorial-capacity or demand data to justify one. A small number of well-evidenced pieces beats a schedule that pushes the Blog back towards evergreen topics. This is a principle, not a measured finding.

## 8. Authorship and trust principles

- **A named author on every post**, with a short profile. Where craftsmanship or gemology is discussed, name the person with relevant experience (workshop lead, designer, gemologist) if HOD has one. Stage 14 decides author and expert profiles for Education; the Blog uses the same profiles.
- **First-hand evidence**: original photography of HOD pieces and the workshop, and process detail only the maker can give. This is the Blog's main advantage over retailer blogs that re-explain the same concepts. INFERENCE from Stage 8/9: the evergreen SERPs are crowded with retailer blogs and guides, while first-hand craft content is scarce.
- **Substantiated claims**: sustainability, sourcing, certification and "made in" claims link to evidence (reports, certificates, policy pages). No unverifiable superlatives.
- **Consent**: client stories need written permission, and personal details are minimised.
- **Commercial disclosure**: launch and gift posts are clearly HOD's own products.

## 9. Internal linking principles

Principles only. The link map is Stage 16.

```
BLOG (editorial, time-bound)
  │  links to the concept it mentions ─────────────► EDUCATION (evergreen owner)
  │  links to the products/collection it features ─► CATEGORY / PRODUCT
  ▼
EDUCATION ── links to the relevant category when the reader is ready to buy ──► CATEGORY
    │                                                                          │
    └── links to a specific product only as an example ──► PRODUCT ◄──────────┘
```

| Link | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Blog → Education** | Whenever a post mentions a concept Education owns | Always link to the **single owner**. Never link to another Blog post for a concept. |
| **Blog → Category** | The post features a product family, style or collection | Link to the live category, not a filtered or parameter URL (Stage 12 D-07) |
| **Blog → Product** | Launches, craft features, client stories about specific pieces | Only for products that are live. Review when a product is retired. |
| **Education → Category** | Buying guides, comparisons and shape/metal pages at the decision point | One or two contextual links to the matching category. Education does not become a product list. |
| **Education → Product** | Only as an illustrative example, such as a shape shown on a real piece | Sparing. Education's authority must not depend on stock. |
| **Education → Blog** | Only where a Blog post adds first-hand evidence (a craft feature showing a setting technique) | Optional, secondary |
| **Category → Education** | Category copy links to the relevant concept owner | Short supporting copy only; no concept re-explanation on category pages (Stage 12 D-06) |
| **FAQ → Education** | Topic questions | One-line answer plus a link to the owner (Stage 12 D-18) |

## 10. Cannibalization prevention rules

These rules preserve the project's evidence standard. **Overlap is not cannibalization.** Cannibalization is claimed only when two HOD URLs are observed competing for the same query or intent. Stage 3 shows no keyword with two HOD URLs ranking, so no cannibalization is currently demonstrated.

1. **One evergreen topic, one owner.** The owner is an Education page, or a legacy `/blog` URL marked Education-owned. Everything else links to it.
2. **Before publishing, check the owner.** If the subject matches an existing owner's cluster (Stage 11), the new piece must take a time-bound or brand angle, or it becomes an update to the owner.
3. **No near-duplicate titles or slug patterns across systems.** Stage 10 found identical "…Complete Buyer's Guide" and brand-suffix patterns in both sections.
4. **Comparison topics have one page per comparison.** GIA vs IGI, lab vs natural and CVD vs HPHT each exist once. A Blog piece may argue a view and link to the comparison.
5. **Split only on SERP evidence.** Two pages for related queries are justified when their SERPs differ, for example the two certification SERPs that share 0 URLs. Two pages are not justified because the wording differs.
6. **Monitor, then act.** If two HOD URLs are later observed ranking for the same query (GSC or a rank check), record it as candidate cannibalization and decide with evidence: consolidate, differentiate or re-link. Do not pre-emptively merge.
7. **The Blog never targets category or product queries.** A launch post never competes with its category page.

## 11. Editorial → Education relationship

- Education is the **reference layer**. The Blog is the **context layer** that sends readers into it.
- A Blog post can **raise** a question, for example "why our workshop prefers a particular setting". Education **answers** the general version, for example setting types.
- When a Blog post keeps drawing evergreen search traffic, its evergreen substance is **promoted** into Education (§6). The Blog does not quietly become a second Education system again.
- Education may cite Blog craft features as **first-hand evidence** (optional link), which strengthens trust signals on reference pages.

## 12. Editorial → Commerce relationship

- The Blog's commercial role is **assisted**: it introduces collections, launches and gifting moments, and links to the live category or product.
- It never replaces a category page. Launch posts point to the collection URL, which remains the page meant to rank for shopping queries [Stage 8: shopping SERPs are CATEGORY/PRODUCT].
- **Bespoke** is the most natural commercial link for editorial content (craft and commission stories lead to the Bespoke service page). INFERENCE, not measured: Stage 11 has no bespoke keywords (keyword-data gap).
- Gift guides link to categories rather than to long product lists, so they survive stock changes.

## 14. Open questions

1. **Editorial capacity.** Who writes and photographs? Named expertise available (designer, workshop lead, gemologist)? This determines what the Blog can credibly publish.
2. **Transitional hub listing.** Should the Blog hub keep listing the Education-owned articles that stay on `/blog` URLs, or should they be linked only from Education? Recommendation: link them from Education as the primary path, and remove them from the Blog hub once the Blog has its own posts. This is a UX choice, not an evidence-based one.
3. **Journal naming.** "Blog" or "Journal"? This has no SEO consequence on current evidence and is a brand choice.
4. **Business facts that gate categories:** natural diamonds, loose stones, gemstones or moissanite, a bespoke commission volume able to support client stories.
5. **Seasonal demand.** Gift and occasion demand is unmeasured. The commercial keyword supplement (Stage 12 D-19) could include seasonal queries if the user approves it.
6. **Platform.** The blog and category URL expression depends on the rebuild platform (Stage 12 D-17).
